Xkot | 12:22 AM

Ten hours after being told that I was going to be getting a pay cut I already had another job lined up. Not just any job. A job doing something I enjoy, working from home, and setting my own schedule. It's so good it almost sounds like a pyramid scheme infomercial, but it's a real job with a real company.


The problem in a situation like this is expressing my joy in a way that doesn't rub salt in the wounds of my unemployed friends. Some of them have been looking for jobs for nine months without success, and here I go quitting one excellent job to go work at another one that's even better. I can't exactly go around grinning like a maniac because I've been so blessed. Instead, when speaking to my jobless friends, I focus more on the part of the story dealing with how my last employer had to lay a bunch of people off and was going to reduce the pay of those of us who were left. My unemployed buddies can sink their teeth into that. The story I tell them is sort of like The Empire Strikes Back. "My hand got cut off, see, and Vader told me he was my dad. My best friend was frozen. Man, it sucked." I leave out the goody-goody Return of the Jedi ending where Vader turns nice and we all cuddle with Ewoks, or in my case, I work in my underwear and sleep as late as I like.

I'm one of those people who tends to go through life feeling like I'm the gum on the bottom of someone's shoe. Even when things are going well I tell myself that I'll screw everything up eventually and it will all come crashing down. I suck all the happiness out of every moment by worrying about what-ifs. Somehow, though, this latest chain of events has gotten through to that inner Eeyore and slapped some sense into him. I'm happy. I don't feel guilty about it. I'm not killing the moment with stress. Yet because I want to be a nice guy and not make my friends feel bad about their situation I'm keeping it all bottled up. Good thing this site is here so I can say it: I am the luckiest man on Earth.
